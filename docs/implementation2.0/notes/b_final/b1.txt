
**Goal.** Teach the model to find off-centre nodules, and use the full learning signal of every step. Do it in this
order: data (B1), numbers (B2), reward (B3).

**Why, with evidence.**

1. The test view is shifted, RL training was not. RL trained on centred views (offset median 0.018 of the side). The
   test view has offset median 0.265 and 37.5 % above 0.3 [measured:
   docs/research/notes/rl_plateau_diagnosis_2026-11-08.md].
2. The misses are where the shift is. Offset = distance between the doctors' union-box centre and the window centre,
   divided by the side S (one definition for all of Phase B) [code: docs/implementation2.0/seeds/b1_scripts/b1_geometry.py].
   Miss = K-mean rule (header). All numbers below are per-item means over K = 8 answers.

   | Split | patients / items | all images IoU | misses | offset < 0.15: n, IoU | offset > 0.3: n, IoU, misses |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | val (all) | 78 / 1,084 | 0.618 | 12.6 % | 206, 0.751 | 406, 0.503, 27.3 % |
   | val-shape (parity rule) | 40 / 449 | 0.645 | 11.6 % | 70, 0.789 | 189, 0.543, 22.2 % |
   | val-select (parity rule) | 38 / 635 | 0.599 | 13.4 % | 136, 0.732 | 217, 0.469, 31.8 % |
   | test | - / 2,200 | 0.645 | 10.8 % | 375, 0.751 | 807, 0.552, 22.2 % |

   [measured: runs/r5/eval/eval_{val,test}/final_eval.json and items.jsonl joined with
   docs/implementation2.0/notes/b1_out/geom.json; val halves: docs/implementation2.0/notes/b_rev/valsplit.json, script
   valsplit.py beside it.] The val halves will be re-dealt by a stratified rule (B1.0); B1.0 re-measures the two half
   rows. The test row is kept as the record of the diagnosis. From 2026-11-08 every design number uses train or val
   only (B.6.2, Limitations).
3. Today's fix (`--train-views`, 24,624 rows) still misses the target law. The 3-view mix has offset median 0.181
   against 0.257 on val (all). Only 47.2 % of its rows have offset above 0.2 (val 68.4 %). Total variation distance
   (TV) of (size x offset) cells to val-select is 0.260, worst cell ratio 0.532 [measured:
   docs/implementation2.0/notes/b_final/trainlaw.json]. The off-centre rows alone (shifted + rescaled, unweighted) give
   worst ratio 0.759, TV 0.108, offset KS 0.109 against the train shifted law (the B1 target, B1.4) [measured: same].
   An earlier test-based check found that no weighting of the 3 fixed windows brings the worst cell ratio to 0.8
   (best try 0:2:1 gives 0.77) [measured on test: docs/implementation2.0/notes/SD3_data.md s5]. The cause: one fixed draw
   per item leaves the small-object, offset 0.15-0.2 cells thin.
4. The data is clean. Patient, series and item overlap across splits are 0. Exact pixel-hash overlap across patients
   is 0. Every doctor mask is inside every window (100 %) [measured: docs/implementation2.0/notes/B1.md F1, F2].
   Two near-duplicate pairs (cos >= 0.98) cross splits: 1 test->train, 1 test->val [measured:
   docs/implementation2.0/notes/b1_out/neardup.json]. DG3 lists and reviews them.
5. Some of the signal is down-weighted or dropped, and nobody knows how much. `importance_sampling_ratio/min` is 0 in
   98.1 % of 770 steps, so at least 1 of 256 sequences was masked in those steps. The median step-mean ratio is 0.82.
   That does not mean 18 % are masked. TRL's `sequence_mask` with `clip_min` None masks only ratios above 3 [code:
   .venv trl/trainer/grpo_trainer.py:2803-2818]. A kept sequence with a summed gap of -3 has weight 0.05. It is
   down-weighted and counted nowhere. The median token |logp diff| is 0.018 [measured: runs/rl/train_rl/rl.json]. The
   mode is `sequence_mask` with cap 3, a TRL default that our config does not set [code:
   src/objdel/train/configs.py:177-196; .venv trl/trainer/grpo_config.py:330-347]. A sequence ratio is exp(sum of
   token gaps), so long answers pass the cap first. The dropped share is not logged [code:
   src/objdel/train/fast.py:351-363, 604-616]. **The mismatch grows with the adapter** [measured: rl.json, by step
   range]:

   | steps | median logp diff | median IS ratio mean | share of steps with IS min = 0 |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 1-20 | 0.011 | 0.998 | 0.25 |
   | 21-100 | 0.028 | 0.827 | 1.00 |
   | 101-300 | 0.024 | 0.771 | 1.00 |
   | 301-500 | 0.019 | 0.810 | 1.00 |
   | 501-770 | 0.016 | 0.845 | 1.00 |

   So a short run from a fresh adapter cannot measure the mismatch. B2.1 logs the counterfactual effect of every IS
   mode inside the B1 run. B2.3b measures the cause (engine kernels, dtype or merged-weight rounding) on drifted
   adapters.
6. A miss gets no gradient toward the nodule. Reward v4 z-scores IoU, F5, legal and g_area inside the group, with
   sigma floors 0.05 / 0.05 / 0.25 / 0.05. A group is zeroed only when every term spans less than 0.01
   [code: src/objdel/reward/advantages.py:95-107; src/objdel/reward/spec.py:28-34]. So an all-miss group still gets
   an advantage from g_area and F5 (for example, doctor-sized versus not). But no v4 term depends on where the
   outline sits once IoU is 0. Misses are 10.8 % of test images (K-mean rule) [measured: final_eval test].
7. The model is probably not the limit, on found images only. Found images score 0.722 against a doctor ceiling of
   0.740 [measured: diagnosis note] [to be confirmed by A2]. The 0.740 is the old ceiling, which A2 calls unfair to
   the doctors. The found subset is also chosen by the model's own success. This bounds the gain on found images
   only; it says nothing about misses. After A2, B.8.4 recomputes the fair difference on val, on found images and on
   all images, with a patient CI.
