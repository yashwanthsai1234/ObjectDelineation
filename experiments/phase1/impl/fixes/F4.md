## B3-1: BinRushed1 model images

**Research.** OpenCV inpainting (Telea fast marching, Navier–Stokes; https://docs.opencv.org/4.x/df/d3d/tutorial_py_inpainting.html):
both propagate the surrounding intensities into the masked region and blur it; for thin regions a small radius is
recommended. A diffusion fill is smoother than the surrounding fundus texture, so an inpainted stroke is still a
smooth band on exactly the answer boundary.

**Sandbox** (`inpaint_sandbox.py`, `riga_trace.py`). Two leak metrics per image, on the full frame:
B3's ink metric (near-black pixels R, G < 80 within 2 px of any rater edge) and a texture-seam metric (median
|Laplacian| of the green channel on the 2-px rater-edge band over the same on a band 8–14 px away).

| subset (old store) | items | ink ≥ 30 px | seam ratio median (range) | seam ≥ 1.5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| BinRushed1 (F1 reconstruction) | 50 | 18 | 3.0 (1.83–6.0) | **50 / 50** |
| BinRushed2 / 3 / 4 | 47 / 47 / 51 | 1 / 0 / 0 | 1.0 (≤ 1.5) | 1 / 0 / 0 |
| MESSIDOR | 460 | 1 | 1.0 (≤ 1.4) | 0 |
| Magrabia F / M | 48 / 47 | 2 / 0 | 1.0 (≤ 1.5) | 5 / 5 (all exactly 1.5) |
| Magrabia F image48 (F7 reconstruction) | 1 | 0 | 1.0 | 0 |

Inpainting the pen-coloured pixels (dilated 2 px, radius 3) on 12 BinRushed1 images: ink on edges falls (e.g.
image012 79 → 11, image003 39 → 1) but the seam ratio does not move at all (Telea and NS identical to the raw image,
2.2–5.5 on 12 / 12). Even BinRushed1 images with 0 ink pixels carry the seam (image006 2.75, image014 2.25): the
per-pixel switch between the six JPEG copies follows every rater's stroke. Inpainting is therefore not a fix.

**Decision.** Drop BinRushed1 from the model-image set (`riga.item`: `image=None` when the reference kind is
`maxrg6`). Masks stay (reward statistics, AB tests); `train.dataset.prepare` already skips items without pixels.
F7 (Magrabia Female image48) measures clean on both metrics and keeps its image, so the Magrabia test set keeps
95 / 95 images.

**After.** No RIGA model image comes from a reconstruction; RIGA items with an image: 547 / 58 / 95
(train / val / test), 700 of 750 (the build report said 94 / 699 until fixes/R1.md §2); BinRushed1 residual ink and seam on rater edges in model images: none (no image). Other subsets are
unchanged (identical bytes).

## B3-2: other annotated nodules in the frame

**Options considered.** (a) Put every nodule the reader drew on that slice into the reader's ground truth: the frame
is cropped around the union of all raters, so the crop grows to all nodules on the slice (often > 300 px apart, scale
> 1 for tiny nodules), the prompt ("Outline the lung nodule", singular) would have to change, and a reader who did not
outline the second nodule would create a fake topology disagreement (the signature counts components). (b) Shrink the
crop: changes M5's frame policy (margins) and still clips nodules that touch the window. (c) Drop such items. (d) Mask
the other nodule out of the CT: an inpainted blob is a new artefact the model can learn.

**Measured** (`lidc_sandbox.py`, all 1,018 series, frames from `objdel.targets.frame`): among 10,841 items left after
P5, another nodule outlined on the same slice overlaps the frame in 163 items when only multi-reader clusters are
counted (B3 counted 114 of these on the prepared train/val subset), **206 with single-reader nodules**, 781 when
< 3 mm nodule centroids are added, 1,155 with non-nodule marks.

**Decision.** (c), with the box rule: an item is not stored when the bounding box of any other nodule outlined on that
slice (any number of readers) intersects its frame box. < 3 mm nodules (one centroid by protocol) and non-nodules
are not counted: the readers' protocol answer to "outline the nodule" never includes them, so the ground truth stays
complete for them. **The prompt does not change.** Stored items carry `meta.other_nodules` so the invariant can be
re-checked against any future frame policy (`test_store_lidc_items_are_distinct_raters_in_a_clear_frame`, now `test_store_lidc_val_rows_keep_the_item_contract`).

**After.** Dropped: 216 items (train 143, val 16, test 57; the box rule is slightly stricter than the 206 pixel rule).
Items whose frame overlaps another outlined nodule: **0** in every split (`verify_store.py`). K5 (targets) is per item
and unaffected; K11 holds (below).

## B3-3: copied outlines

**Research.** The LIDC protocol: in the unblinded phase each reader reviewed their own marks together with the
anonymised marks of the others and could keep, delete or reclassify their own marks or add marks; no forced consensus
(Armato et al. 2007, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2151472 ; Armato et al. 2011,
https://doi.org/10.1118/1.3528204). pylidc's documentation, issue tracker and the DICOM re-encoding paper (Fedorov
et al. 2020, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7721965/) do not mention identical reader contours; the
LIDC-IDRI-Lite notes cover duplicate z, not duplicate reads. Not a documented artefact, so the XML was audited
(`dup_nodules.py`): 160 nodule pairs from two sessions share at least one point-for-point identical contour (> 4
points), 29 of them every ROI; **158 / 160 carry different characteristics** and every pair has different nodule ids.
So these are not duplicated sessions but outlines one reader adopted from another (the reader still rated the
nodule independently). Of 425 identical slice masks, 392 come from identical contours; the other 33 are all ≥ 20 px.

**Decision.** Deduplicate (keep the lowest session number; `meta.copied_raters`): the reward treats raters as
independent draws (τ = P95 of rater-pair distances, T5 calibration, topology frequencies), and a copied outline is
not a second outline. Rule P5 in `lidc.slices` is on mask identity, the object the reward sees.

**Effect on rater counts and rl-eligibility** (old → new store; every stored item has k ≥ 2, so all stay rl-eligible):

| | k = 2 | k = 3 | k = 4 | items |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| old store | 2,867 | 2,826 | 5,228 | 10,921 |
| new store | 2,847 | 2,861 | 4,917 | 10,625 |

338 layers removed in 334 stored items (train 225, val 37, test 72); 80 slices drop below two distinct readers and are
no longer items (they were "disagreement-free" 2-reader copies). Identical layers in the new store: 0.

## B3-8: same-eye photographs across splits (K11)

**Research.** Near-duplicate image detection under rotation/scale is classically keypoints + spatial verification
(Philbin et al. 2007, https://www.robots.ox.ac.uk/~vgg/publications/2007/Philbin07/philbin07.pdf; Lowe's ratio test,
https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~lowe/papers/ijcv04.pdf); fundus pairs of one eye are registered from keypoint
correspondences (FIRE benchmark, https://doi.org/10.35119/maio.v1i4.42; SIFT/vessel features do best there, ORB is the
fast binary option in OpenCV, https://docs.opencv.org/4.x/d1/d89/tutorial_py_orb.html). Alternative considered:
search the NCC over a rotation × scale grid: 81× the cost and a larger null maximum.

**Sandbox.**
1. Feature count (`orb_sandbox.py`, 37 known duplicate pairs, the breach pair, 3,058 other pairs): 500 / 1,000 /
   1,500 features give duplicate minima 95 / 176 / 207 and unrelated maxima 19 / 27 / 27 inliers, at 1.5 / 4.9 /
   8.9 ms per pair (homography; similarity is no better). Chosen: 500.
2. First build (no field mask, threshold 50): found 10 new pairs, but the all-pairs scan (`orb_gap.py`) showed
   same-eye pairs down to 43 inliers and different-eye pairs up to 34: the gap was not clean.
3. **Field mask.** Unrelated pairs scored 20–27 inliers at an identity transform: the camera's circular field edge
   and its notch match across every photograph. Detecting only inside the field shrunk by 20 px (`riga.keypoints`)
   drops those to ≤ 6 while keeping the true pairs (`orb_mask.py`).
4. **Calibration over all 280,875 pairs** (`orb_gap2.py`): every pair with ≥ 8 inliers (512) labelled independently by
   the correlation of high-pass CLAHE-green vessel maps after a 1,500-feature similarity alignment (and by eye on
   overlays for the four lowest same-eye pairs, `overlay*.jpg`): **same eye 51 pairs, ≥ 29 inliers (vessel correlation
   0.30–0.95); different eyes ≤ 16 inliers (correlation ≤ 0.07).** Threshold `ORB_DUP = 22`. The lowest same-eye
   pairs are re-photographs with a large fixation change (BinRushed3/042 ↔ BinRushed4/036: 29; BinRushed2/003 ↔ 004:
   35; MESSIDOR 325 ↔ 447: 42; BinRushed2/037 ↔ BinRushed3/003: 47).

**After (final build).** 51 near-duplicate pairs (37 NCC, all of them also keypoint pairs with ≥ 106 inliers; 14
keypoint-only), 45 groups. Cross-split: 0 NCC pairs (max 0.326), **0 keypoint pairs (max cross-split inliers 16;
max of all pairs below the threshold 16)**, 0 group leaks. BinRushed3/image004 + 026 and BinRushed4/image002 are one
group in one split (`test_store_k11_keypoints`, slow, passes on `data/store_new`). Magrabia: the test split keeps its
95 ids; the two new Magrabia pairs (MagrabiFemale 014 + 038, 023 + 045, both inside test) lower the unique test eyes
from 88 to **86** (the plan's "88 photos" figure becomes 86). Build time 827 s with 2 workers (pass 1 376 s, of
which the all-pairs ORB scan in 36 block jobs), peak RSS of the main process 1.96 GB.

## I20: store.load memory

`pyarrow.parquet.ParquetFile(pre_buffer=True)` (the default) reads whole column chunks of a row group ahead
(https://arrow.apache.org/docs/python/generated/pyarrow.parquet.ParquetFile.html). Peak RSS of a full RIGA val pass
(`mem_load.py`): 16-row batches 1,543 MB; 4 rows 933 MB; 1 row 753 MB; **1 row with `pre_buffer=False` 455 MB**, at the
same speed (0.4–0.8 s for 64 RIGA rows; LIDC val 1,085 rows 0.57 s vs 0.64 s). Chosen: `BATCH_ROWS = 1`,
`pre_buffer=False`.

## I10: RIGA 591 / 64 / 95

`riga_split` deals 10 % of the duplicate *groups* of each subset stratum by largest remainder over the seeded hash
order of group ids, and a group goes to val with all its photographs. The photo counts therefore move with the
grouping and with the per-subset rounding (±1 group). The NCC grouping gave 591 / 64; with the B3-8 keypoint groups
the same rule gives 592 / 63 / 95 (an intermediate build with fewer keypoint groups gave the plan's 590 / 65).
Both are correct draws of the rule; the per-subset val counts are within one group of 10 % (`val_by_subset`:
BinRushed1–4 5 / 4 / 5 / 5, MESSIDOR 44). Changing the rule to deal photographs instead of groups was not worth a
new split algorithm in this wave. Recorded in `build_report.riga` (`split_note`, `val_by_subset`, `groups_by_subset`).
