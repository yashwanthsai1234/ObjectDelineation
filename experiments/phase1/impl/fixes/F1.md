## B1-1 parse whitespace

- **Fix.** `text.strip(" \t\n\r")`.
  - Leading Unicode spaces (U+3000, U+2028, U+00A0, U+0085) now give `not_ascii`.
  - `\x0b`, `\x0c` and `\x1c`–`\x1f` give `whitespace`.
  - A leading NUL gives `syntax`; a trailing NUL gives `trailing_text`.
- **Sources.**
  - RFC 8259 §2: insignificant whitespace is exactly `%x20 / %x09 / %x0A / %x0D`. https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc8259#section-2
  - `str.strip()` with no argument strips every `str.isspace()` character. https://docs.python.org/3/library/stdtypes.html#str.strip

## I4 parse length cap and huge integers

**Measurement** (`chars_per_token.py`). The three tokenizers ran on 3,050 SFT targets (a random sample plus the 50 longest) and on 5 adversarial grammar texts (1,000 points of `1000`, `0`, `100`, random points; 300 tiny holes). Maximum characters per token:

| tokenizer | max chars/token |
|---|---|
| LFM2.5-VL | 2.14 (median 2.07) |
| Qwen3-VL | 1.70 |
| SmolVLM | 1.60 |

- **Completion size.** A 1,536-token completion therefore has at most about 3,300 characters, and a 1,024-token LFM completion about 2,200.
- **Targets.** The longest of the 31,922 targets in `data/targets` has 2,864 characters.

**Choice: `MAX_CHARS = 16_384`.**
- It is 5× the densest 1,536-token completion and 5.7× the longest target.
- It would still cover a completion budget of about 7,600 tokens.
- With it, `parse("x" * 10**7)` is O(1) (< 1 ms, tested). Before, a 600 kB text took 40 ms.
- `build_target` parses its own text, so a target longer than 16,384 characters would now raise. None exists: the longest has 2,864.

**Huge integers.**
- Before: Python's int-string limit (4,300 digits, https://docs.python.org/3/library/stdtypes.html#int-max-str-digits) raised `ValueError` inside `json.loads`, and `_diagnose` reported `syntax`.
- Fix: `_diagnose` now passes `parse_int=_int` (https://docs.python.org/3/library/json.html). Any literal of 6+ characters, sign included, is outside [0, 1000] anyway, so it maps to GRID + 1 without calling `int()`.
- 5-, 4,300-, 4,301- and 10,000-digit integers (positive, negative, `10…0`) all report `range`.
- The `except ValueError` branch for huge ints is gone.

Two existing tests changed:
- the `"[" * 100_000` rejection is now `"[" * 10_000` (code `syntax`); the 100 k case is `too_long`;
- the linear-time adversarial tests now use the largest text under the cap (> MAX_CHARS / 4), with a bound of 20 ms instead of 100 ms.

## B1-2 polycore.pack shapes

**Fix.** `_as_i32(a, ndim)` accepts:
- `(k, 2)` for vertices;
- 1-D for offsets and roles;
- `(0,)` / `(0, 2)` as empty.

Anything else raises `ValueError` and is never reshaped. A tuple of two `(k, 2)` rings is no longer mistaken for a packed pair: its `off` is 2-D, so it raises `ValueError` up front.

**Not changed.** Non-integer dtypes still raise `TypeError`, and values outside int32 raise `OverflowError` (existing contract and tests; the B1 reproducer accepts both). That is why the contract line says `TypeError` for non-integers.

## I1 / B1-5 / B2-10 mask normalisation

**Research and sandbox** (`boolbytes.py`, numpy 2.5.4).
- These keep the raw bytes of a bool array: bool → bool copies such as `astype(bool)` (with or without `copy=False`), `np.asarray(m, bool)`, `np.ascontiguousarray(m, bool)`, `np.array(m, bool)` and `np.pad`.
- Every ufunc and every cast to or from another dtype normalises: `!= 0`, `&`, `~`, `astype(uint8)`, `sum`, `packbits`, `count_nonzero`.
- The numpy source confirms the casts: `_CONVERT_FN(x)` is `(npy_bool)(x != 0)` whenever the source or the destination is bool. https://github.com/numpy/numpy/blob/main/numpy/_core/src/multiarray/lowlevel_strided_loops.c.src
- JAX documents the same pitfall and recommends `!= 0`: https://docs.jax.dev/en/latest/_autosummary/jax.Array.view.html

So the two pitfalls are exactly `.view(np.uint8)` and `1 - mp` on a bool array that was only bool→bool copied. That is what `checker.topology` did.

**Cost** (`bench_norm.py`, best of 5, µs; unloaded):

| option | 50×50 | 500×500 | correct |
|---|---|---|---|
| `np.ascontiguousarray(m, np.uint8)` (cast) | 0.32 | 5.2 | yes |
| `np.not_equal(m, 0)` | 1.25 | 52.7 | yes |
| crop on raw bytes, then `np.minimum(crop, 1)` | 2.1 | 23–40 | yes |
| old `ascontiguousarray(m, bool)` + crop | 1.5 | 20 | **no** |
| `instance_topology` (reference) | 6.3 | 166 | — |

**Decision: `as_mask`.**
- bool input: cast to uint8 and view back (10× cheaper than `!= 0`);
- other dtypes: `ascontiguousarray(m, bool)`, a value cast `x != 0` in which NaN counts as foreground, as before;
- input whose ndim is not the expected one raises `ValueError`;
- it always returns a new array.

Overhead: about 3–6 % of `instance_topology`; under load, 1.1 / 15.7 µs against 18.6 / 1,192 µs (`bench_topo.py`).

**Where it is called.**
- topology: `instance_topology` (hence `signature` and `betti`) and `size_filter`, once per call; `_crop_pad` now takes a normalised mask.
- `build_target`: `src`.
- `frame`: every rater stack, with `ndim=3`, so frame masks, and therefore targets and caches built from them, are canonical.

**Untouched.**
- `bitquads`, `polygonize` and `certify` were already correct: they use pad + cast, `~`, `&` and `np.any`. `test_non_canonical_bool_bytes` pins that.
- `polycore._mask` and the C++ `gt[p] != 0` were already correct.

**ExpertCache (reward code, not edited).** B2-10 now passes because `ExpertCache` computes `rater_sigs` through `signature`. Its other byte reads:
- `boundary` casts to uint8;
- `_band` sums;
- `band_depth` uses `&`/`~`;
- `boundingRect` treats any nonzero byte as foreground.

All of these were checked to normalise. One thing remains (for F2 / the coordinator): `ExpertCache.raters` itself still stores the caller's bytes, and so does the saved `.npz`.
- Where: `src/objdel/reward/cache.py:153` (`__init__`), `:176` (`build`), `:192` (`load`, `astype(bool)` keeps bytes).
- Suggestion: use `as_mask(stack, 3)` there so the stored stack is canonical.
- `score.py:74` (`score_mask`) is harmless: `boundingRect` treats any nonzero byte as foreground, and every later use normalises.

## B1-3 default min_area

**Problem.** The reward filters at `min_area(|U|)` of the raters' union. A single-rater call cannot know |U|, only that |U| lies between the mask's own area and the crop (H·W). Signature equality at one rule implies nothing at a larger one. The signature is not even monotone in the rule: a 28-px moat around a 36-px island is filled at rule 30, but at rule 40 the island is removed first and the hole, now 64 px, survives (`test_kept_sees_a_hole_freed_by_a_removed_island`).

**Options considered.**
1. Make `min_area` required. Every production caller already passes it (`train/dataset.py:81` and `build_targets.py:51`, both with `"rim"`). But the B1-3/B1-4/B1-5 reproducers, `test_targets_on_degenerate_and_rectangular_crops` (break-it, not mine), many `test_targets` cases and `eval_demo.py` / `tests/test_eval*.py` (without `"rim"`) call the default. The reproducers must pass unchanged, so this was rejected.
2. **Adopted: a safe default.** A name without an entry is certified at every rule in `[min_area(own), min_area(H·W)]`.

**How `_kept` stays cheap.**
- The opening at rule t depends only on which raw components have ≥ t px.
- Given the opening, the closing depends only on which holes of the opened mask have ≥ t px. Holes ≥ t survive into the filtered mask with their size unchanged, and holes < t stay filled at every larger rule.
- So the filtered mask is constant between consecutive values of {raw component size + 1} ∪ {filtered hole size + 1}. `_kept` checks one rule per such interval, typically 2–5 checks.
- When the rule is given (`lo == hi`), `_kept` is the old `signature(a, t) == signature(b, t)`, with the same calls and the same cost. Production texts are therefore unchanged.

**Evidence.**
- `_kept` equals the brute force over every integer rule on 300 Hypothesis pairs of masks (independent, near-copies, filtered copies).
- B1's random search (`p11.py`) re-run as `p11_rerun.py`: seeds 1 and 2, 120 masks each, 500 px blobs with 1–4 holes. Every built target is checked by brute force at rules [own, own + 60], as B1 did, and by `_kept` over the whole range up to min_area(250,000) = 1,250. Result:
  - **0 mismatches** among the 111 targets built (seed 1: 60, seed 2: 51); B1 found 15 / 240 on the same masks.
  - Build CPU median 0.25 / 0.28 s, max 0.58 s.
  - 141 / 240 masks have more than 10 raw rings. Before, all of them raised `MAX_RINGS`, and B1's script skipped them.
    - 17 are now certified, through the B1-4 path.
    - 124 are still refused: 110 fail IoU ≥ 0.99 once the size filter removes their sub-rule holes (> 1 % of the area), and 14 still have more than 10 rings after the filter.
  - The other **5 / 240 (2.1 %) are new refusals of the safe default.** Example: seed 2 #76 has holes of 482 and 492 px inside the rule range [487, 1,250], so they must be pixel-exact; the lossless rings then exceed 1,000 vertices. No answer within the caps keeps the topology at every possible union rule. Refusing is the documented failure mode, and the old default certified a target that is wrong for part of that range. With the reward's rule passed, the same mask is certified (τ = 1.25, 892 vertices).
- The B1-3 reproducer (371-px hole, own rule 352): the default target now rasterizes the hole as exactly 371 px. With the reward's rule passed, the text is shorter (tested).
- **Cost:** the default can need lower rungs (a hole whose size lies inside the rule range must be reproduced to the pixel).

## B1-4 ring cap after the size rule

- **Fix.** Rings are traced from the raw mask, as before. Only when there are more than `MAX_RINGS` are they traced from `size_filter(mask, rule)`, where rule is the given one or the mask's own `min_area`. Certification is unchanged: the raster is compared with the raw mask (IoU ≥ 0.99, size-filtered signature).
- **Error.** Still more than 10 rings after the filter: `ValueError("... rings > MAX_RINGS = 10 after the size rule")`.
- **Evidence.**
  - A disc with ten 1-px pinholes and twelve 1-px specks (23 raw rings) gets a one-ring target, both with rule 5 and with the default.
  - A box with eleven 6-px holes still raises at rule 5.
  - Source: the size rule as area opening/closing (Vincent 1994, already cited in `checker/topology.py`).
- **Production unaffected.** Every record in `data/prepared/*/*.jsonl` has `errors: []`, and `data/targets/*.jsonl` has no error rows. So no production mask hit the cap, and the production texts and the target-set hashes are unchanged.

## B1-6 frame image layouts

- **Fix.** Accepted layouts are `(H, W)`, `(H, W, 1)`, `(H, W, 3)` and `(H, W, 4)`. Alpha is dropped (`[..., :3]`), gray is repeated ×3, and anything else raises `ValueError("... gray, RGB or RGBA ...")`.
- **Alpha.** It is dropped, not composited: the stores hold opaque fundus photos and CT slices, so there is nothing to blend against.
- **Tests.** Exact pixel check against a direct crop for all four layouts; the refused layouts are 2-, 5- and 0-channel, 4-D and 1-D.

## I8 exceptions in score_batch workers

**Research.**
- An exception that escapes a `std::thread` function calls `std::terminate`: https://en.cppreference.com/w/cpp/thread/thread/thread
- `std::exception_ptr` / `std::current_exception` / `std::rethrow_exception` carry it to another thread: https://en.cppreference.com/w/cpp/error/exception_ptr
- `join()` synchronizes with the end of the thread, so reading each worker's slot after joining is race-free.
- nanobind translates `std::bad_alloc` to `MemoryError`: https://nanobind.readthedocs.io/en/latest/exceptions.html
- A thread that cannot be created (`std::system_error`) left the vector holding joinable threads during unwinding, which also calls `std::terminate`. This is the "terminate called without an active exception" seen below.

**Fix** (`cpp/polycore/polycore.hpp`, `score_batch`, about 12 lines):
- one `exception_ptr` per worker; a worker that catches sets `next = n`, so the others stop at their next rollout;
- the main thread joins all workers, then rethrows the first exception;
- a `std::system_error` from `emplace_back` leaves the batch to the threads already started;
- no hook and no test-only code.

**Test** (Linux only: macOS does not enforce `RLIMIT_AS`, and `setrlimit` there fails).
- A subprocess sets `RLIMIT_AS` to its current size + 24 MB and scores 8 cap-sized zigzag shapes at 4096², each needing 41 MB of key scratch.
- It expects `MemoryError` with threads 1, 4 and 64.
- It runs with `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1`. In a core dump of a flaky first version, OpenCV's bundled OpenBLAS 0.3.15 jumped to a null pointer in `blas_memory_alloc` inside a `blas_thread_server` thread when that thread's lazy buffer allocation failed (2–27 % of runs), whatever polycore did.
- The limit is restored in `finally`, so interpreter shutdown is not at the limit.

**Evidence** (Docker `polycore-linux:arm64`, Python 3.12, g++, the old and new trees built from the same sources):

| build | threads 1 | 2 | 4 | 8 | 64 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| old | 30/30 MemoryError | 30/30 SIGABRT | 30/30 SIGABRT | 30/30 SIGABRT | 30/30 SIGABRT |
| new | 30/30 MemoryError | 30/30 MemoryError | 30/30 MemoryError | 30/30 MemoryError | 30/30 MemoryError |

- The whole `tests/test_polycore.py` passes in the container (41 tests).
- `make fuzz FUZZ_N=3000` (Apple clang, ASan+UBSan, `-Werror`) is clean.
- g++ 14 in `gcc:14` (ASan+UBSan, `-Werror`), 1,500 iterations, is clean.

## I14 cv2.setNumThreads

- **Re-verified on this machine** (opencv-python-headless 5.0.0, parallel framework GCD): `setNumThreads(1)`, `(2)`, `(4)`, `(8)` and `(-1)` all leave `getNumThreads() == 8`; `setNumThreads(0)` gives 1.
- **OpenCV docs:** "If threads == 0, OpenCV will disable threading optimizations and run all its functions sequentially". https://docs.opencv.org/5.x/db/de0/group__core__utils.html
- **Corrected:**
  - the `M3_topology.md` open-issue line;
  - the `checker/topology.py` docstring (new "Threads" paragraph);
  - `test_topology.py`'s determinism test, which compared "1 vs 4", i.e. 8 vs 8 threads on macOS, and now compares 0 vs 4.

- **Left for the owner** of `docs/research/notes/Phase 1 stack and algorithms/` (not F1): `mask_algorithms.md` line 5 and `prototypes/bench_mask.py` line 10 still say `setNumThreads(1)`. Those benchmarks therefore ran with 8 OpenCV threads on macOS.
